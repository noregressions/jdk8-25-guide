Steve Poole has been working on Java SDKs and JVMs since Java was less than a year old. He spent the bulk of his career at IBM building and shipping Java runtimes, representing IBM on various Java Specification Requests, and committing to Apache, Eclipse, and OpenJDK projects along the way. He was an early member of the Adopt OpenJDK group, campaigning for community involvement in OpenJDK back when that was a slightly unusual thing to want.

He is a Java Champion, an Oracle ACE, an IBM Champion, and a director of the London Java Community. He has spent years on stage at JavaOne, Devoxx, JAX, and QCon, explaining JVM internals to rooms full of people who'd generally rather the JVM just behaved itself.

Steve is now a Developer Advocate at HeroDevs, where he spends his time on the software supply chain problem from every angle: end-of-life dependencies, AI-assisted development, and the unglamorous, unfunded work of keeping ageing platforms secure. This guide sits squarely in that territory. A JDK migration is, underneath the syntax errors and the stack traces, a supply chain problem: it just happens to be the one dependency you can't fork.

---

## Why this guide exists

I've watched this exact migration happen at enough organisations, in enough different guises, to notice the pattern: the technical work is rarely the hard part. The hard part is not knowing what you don't know. Teams stall not because the fixes are difficult, but because nobody can say with confidence how big the problem is until they've already started.

This guide is my attempt to close that gap. Thirty-eight failure categories, mapped and verified against real JDK 8 and JDK 25 builds, organised so a taxonomy stands in for guesswork. If it saves one team a week of `NoClassDefFoundError` archaeology, it's done its job.
