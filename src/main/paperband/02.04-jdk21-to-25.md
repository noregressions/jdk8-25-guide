The final span contains what the talk calls the second wall: JDK 24, where the Security Manager's removal lands as three distinct breakages in one release, alongside the start of the Unsafe and JNI warning regimes. JDK 25 then contributes its own novelties — compact headers, `java.lang.IO`, class-file version 69.

**If this is your hop** — and for readers doing 8→25 in one move, this is the wall you hit *after* fixing everything earlier — three campaigns matter. First, the Security Manager triage: audit for all three failure shapes at once (flags, calls, and the silent checks of 3.16, which is the one that can't be found by waiting for errors). Second, the warning-phase evictions: Unsafe and native access warn today and break on a future release, and both have simulate-tomorrow flags (`--sun-misc-unsafe-memory-access=deny`, `--illegal-native-access=deny`) that belong in CI now. Third, the JDK 25 recompile novelties: the `IO` collision and the class-file floor are trial-compile discoveries — cheap if you look, baffling if you don't.

**Beyond 25**, two shipped facts and one commitment: JDK 26 removed `Thread.stop()` entirely and the Applet API with it (3.10, 3.33), and the Unsafe deny-by-default change remains committed by JEP 498 — 26 shipped still warn-only, which is why the `=deny` CI flag above matters.

Tools for this span: `jnativescan` (1.3) and the Unsafe flag ladder (1.7) are purpose-built for it; the flag audit (1.5) catches the Security Manager switch.
