The 11→17 span is when warnings became walls. Reflective access to JDK internals — warned about since 9 — became deny-by-default in 16 and permanently enforced in 17. Around it, the platform shed major components: CMS, Nashorn, Pack200, RMI Activation, biased locking.

**If this is your hop:** one item towers over the rest — strong encapsulation (3.5). Every JDK-8-era framework version that reflects into JDK internals stops working between 16 and 17, and the fix hierarchy is always the same: upgrade the framework, or targeted `--add-opens` with a tracking issue. The component removals (CMS, Nashorn, Pack200, RMI Activation) are binary questions — you either use them or you don't, and `jdeps` plus a grep answers each in minutes. JEP 411 is the span's time bomb rather than its explosion: nothing breaks at 17, but the Security Manager's whole failure family (3.2, 3.8, 3.16) is now scheduled.

Tools for this span: `jdeps --jdk-internals` (1.1) is the headline detector; `-Xlog:exceptions=info` (1.7) catches the reflective failures frameworks swallow; the flag audit (1.5) catches the CMS carriers.
